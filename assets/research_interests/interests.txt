The most prominent part of my studies was building a Recommendation system based on Deep Learning in my final year which could handle the challenges such as faced by traditional collaborative filtering(CF) approaches. While CF approach is the most widely used approach due to its effectiveness, they still have limitations such as coping with data sparsity, cold-start problems, and linearity assumptions in matrix factorization (MF). To handle these limitations, Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) have emerged as a powerful alternative, addressing these limitations by learning non-linear user-item interactions and automatically extracting high-level features from raw data. The results were surprising in that the DNN model was able to capture non-linear relationships in sparse data and another significant achievement was its ability to leverage implicit feedback (user clicks, purchase history). In this project I was introduced to the world of recommender systems and their impact on users purchase behavior that enables them make better decisions and find the item/content of their interest easily. The related publications helped me understand how we can switch to a hybrid model such as Neural Collaborative Filtering by X. He and also the impact of various parameters such as batches, weights, and bias during training and tuning a neural model on a dataset.
